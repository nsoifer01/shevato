# Trip Planner

**A day-by-day itinerary builder: log flights, stays and activities, watch costs and night coverage add up, get warned about date collisions and uncovered nights, see the whole route on a map, and compare ways to get from A to B.**

## How it works

Everything lives in `localStorage` under the `trip-planner:*` keys and works without an account. Signing in on the site (Firebase Auth, shared header) additionally syncs `trip-planner:v1`, `trip-planner:timefmt`, `trip-planner:distunit` and `trip-planner:tempunit` across devices through the shared `sync-system/` (Firestore namespace `tripPlannerApp`); the geocode and venue-coordinate caches stay device-local on purpose. A trip is a named list of items (`flight | stay | transport | local | activity | note`, where `local` is travel within a city), each with dates, optional times (including overnight arrivals like SHV to HND landing +1d), a status (`Booked / To book / Decide later / Cancelled`), and an optional cost (which may be negative to record a refund or credit). An activity may also carry a `meal` category (`breakfast | brunch | lunch | dinner | drinks | cafe | snack | other`), which is what the form's **Food & Drink** type stores: the storage schema deliberately keeps six types and adds a FIELD rather than a seventh type, because every already-deployed copy of the app coerces an unknown type to `note` and sync is whole-db last-writer-wins, so one stale tab would destroy both the category and the type - an unknown field rides through untouched. Every edit re-renders the summary chips, the night-coverage strip, the warnings panel and the totals instantly.

Most network calls are opt-in and key-free: place lookup via OpenStreetMap Nominatim (cached in `localStorage`, 1 request/second), city suggestions in the place fields via Open-Meteo's geocoding endpoint (a separate provider from Nominatim on purpose: Nominatim's usage policy forbids autocomplete against its public instance), typical weather from Open-Meteo, daily exchange rates from Frankfurter, the visa dataset from GitHub, and map tiles via OpenStreetMap. hotel suggestions on stay items and venue suggestions on activity items via Photon (OSM data again, but a separate provider from Nominatim for the same reason the city fields use Open-Meteo: Nominatim's usage policy forbids autocomplete, and Photon is built for it). The airport picker makes no network call at all: its table ships with the app under `data/`, and neither does booking import, which reads a confirmation entirely on the device. Leaflet itself is self-hosted under `vendor/leaflet/` (no CDN), loaded on demand the first time the Map view opens. The one keyed, billed call is Google venue ratings. On assistant suggestion cards they appear as a separate star-rating chip; on itinerary items with a Maps place (Timeline and Days views) the score and review count are merged into the item's own Google Maps link, so one element reads `Google Maps · ⭐ 4.7 (1,800)` and opens the resolved place. The same response now also carries the venue's opening hours (both hours fields sit in the same Place Details Enterprise SKU the rating already bills, so they cost nothing extra and add no request): activity rows in the Days view show a compact `Hours · 4:00 PM–11:00 PM` line for their own scheduled day, and every timed assistant venue suggestion is checked deterministically against those hours - a proposal whose start time falls at or after closing (or on a closed day) is visibly demoted as `Closed at ...`, one that falls BEFORE the doors open says so instead (`Opens at 6:00 PM`, because a 5:30 booking at a bar that opens at 6 needs a later hour rather than another venue), one that is technically open but leaves less than its venue type's minimum recommendation window before closing (30 min for meals and cafes and shops, 45 for drinks and galleries, 60 for museums, 45 otherwise) is demoted in amber as `Closes at 11:00 PM · only 20 min remaining`, and BOTH states are excluded from the pick-one winner badges and cannot be accepted unchanged: the accept is refused with the verified hours quoted, and the offered way forward is the item form, prefilled, where the traveller changes the time and whatever they save is a manual item of their own. A venue with verified hours is never accepted as a timed assistant recommendation at a time outside those hours; only a deliberate manual edit can schedule against a listing, and the Days view then flags it in red. Hours that cannot be verified (no key, quota spent, offline, Google has none) simply paint nothing: unknown means UNVERIFIED, and unverified is never presented as open - the app never claims every venue is checked when Places data is unavailable. A travel LEG never gets a rating, on a card or a row: a rating answers "is this worth going to", which is a question about a venue you are choosing between, and nobody is choosing the ride home to a hotel they already booked. A leg that names a real destination gets a `Directions` action instead, opening Google Maps directions from where the leg actually starts in the mode its own distance implies (walking for a short hop, transit otherwise, driving between cities); on Timeline, which has no day chain to ask, the same link is destination-only and lets Maps ask for the starting point. That also spends nothing: a leg makes no Places call at all. Every place a traveller can walk into qualifies, not just the ones the assistant tagged: an item with no `mapsQuery` of its own derives one from its title and location, for stays and activities only (a flight, a between-cities leg, a local hop and a note are not places, so they derive nothing). It stays dormant until the owner configures a Places key (see below); with no key the assistant chips stay empty and invisible while the itinerary link simply reads `Google Maps` with no rating, at zero cost. Offline, the timeline works fully; the map, route lookup, weather and ratings degrade with clear messages. Data sources are credited on the page, with a note that none of them are official sources for entry requirements.

## Features

| Feature | What it does |
| ------- | ------------ |
| Timeline | Chronological board of all items with per-night rates, status pills (quick-change), duplicate/delete, an edit action, and a collapsible stay/day/item hierarchy; filters and search apply here and in Days view. Items with a Maps place show a single Google Maps link that carries the star rating and review count inline (`Google Maps · ⭐ 4.7 (1,800)`, upgrading its href to the resolved place) once ratings are configured, and a plain `Google Maps` link otherwise; the same combined link appears in Days view |
| Bulk actions | "Select items" in the toolbar puts the Timeline into selection mode: every row matching the current filters gets a checkbox, whether it sits at the top level or folded inside a stay, and a bar above the board reads "{n} selected" alongside Select all, a status control and Delete selected. A status change or a delete across the whole selection is one undo step. Parent and child are independent picks, so ticking a stay never reaches inside it and ticking every item inside one never ticks the stay; Select all expands any collapsed group holding a selection so a pick is never invisible; and a stay drawn only because something inside it matches still gets no checkbox of its own. With nothing selected both actions are disabled, and leaving selection mode, switching view or touching any filter clears the selection; a second picker CONVERTS the selected items' costs into another currency at the day's rates behind a confirm that names the count, the target and anything it cannot convert (which keeps its own currency and amount untouched), one undo step |
| Undo / redo | Every data change (add, edit, delete, duplicate, shift, status, import, trip ops) is undoable 50 steps deep: toolbar buttons or Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y; history resets when a remote sync merge lands |
| Multiple trips | Create, rename, duplicate and delete trips; per-trip currency (whatever the rates provider currently quotes, read from the live rate payload so the picker and the conversion can never disagree, falling back to a checked-in list until rates load; the 8 common ones are grouped first, and any code a trip already uses that the provider no longer prices is kept in its own "Saved with this trip (no live rate)" group so opening a picker never rewrites saved data) switchable from the totals footer picker, with the symbol shown in the Cost column header and as a prefix inside the Cost input |
| Trip essentials | 🆘 Trip essentials in the trip menu keeps the "if something goes wrong" details a buried note item cannot: emergency contact name and phone, insurance provider and policy phone, and free-text medical notes, all optional and all per trip. Blank on every field reads "Nothing saved yet" rather than an empty form, and the menu entry never carries a badge. It rides along in JSON export and Backup all trips and is restored on re-import, and is deliberately kept OUT of CSV, calendar (.ics) and share links: these are the details you least want to hand to someone else along with an itinerary. Deleting a trip takes its essentials with it |
| Search across trips | A 🔎 next to the trip picker searches item titles, places, details and confirmation codes across EVERY saved trip at once, not just the open one, answering "which trip did I put that booking code in". Up to 20 matches list title, date and which trip they belong to, sorted by trip then date, with a "+N more" line past that; picking one switches trips, switches to Timeline, and flashes the row using the same jump-to-row highlight the warnings panel uses. It is hidden entirely on a read-only shared trip, where there is only one trip to search and it is not yours |
| Validation | Blocks bad input (missing title, check-out before check-in, arrival before departure, calendar-impossible or out-of-range dates); negative costs are accepted deliberately as refunds; timezone-aware: a flight may land the same day at an "earlier" local time. A single far-future date can no longer hang the app: day walks stop at a cap and the offending item is named as an error. A double-click on Save (or two rapid Enter presses) adds ONE item, not two: the second submit lands after the first has already saved and closed the dialog, so it is ignored rather than read as a second, independently valid item |
| Warnings | Date collisions (two stays covering the same night), uncovered nights up to the trip's end (a trip with no stay AT ALL reports its own nights, which is the one trip where every night is uncovered and the panel used to say nothing beside a chip counting "0 of 4 nights booked"), past items still "To book"; click-through from the warnings panel to the row. Also flags an impossible connection (a flight/transport/local leg that departs at or before the previous leg arrives) and a tight one (a positive gap under 45 minutes, stated in minutes); legs more than 24 hours apart, a leg with no time, or a hotel night landing between two legs are never treated as a connection. What is compared is the two consecutive LEGS, not two adjacent rows: anything else logged in between - a note on the departure day, a coffee at the airport, the dinner booked for that evening - leaves the connection exactly as tight as it was, where it used to silence the warning altogether. And it nudges when two non-cancelled items share the exact same date and clock time, worded as a double-check rather than a certainty. Every such warning names both items so the jump-to-row link still works. It also looks BEYOND the open trip: if another saved trip covers any of the same calendar days, a line names it and its dates ("Overlaps with another trip: "Lisbon long weekend" (Jun 8 - Jun 15, 2027)"), computed from whichever trip you are looking at so opening either side names the other. The other trip's name is a link that switches to it and leaves your filters alone; trips that merely touch (one ends the day the next begins) raise nothing, and a trip with no dated items can neither raise this nor be named by it |
| Booking deadlines | Any item takes an optional "Book by" date under Status, for the bookings that have to happen before the trip date itself (prices spike, seats sell out). While the item is still "To book" the warnings panel counts it down inside a 7-day window as "book by {date}, 3 days left" (or "today" on the day itself), and calls it out as "booking deadline passed ({date})" once it is behind; both click through to the row like every other warning. Marking the item Booked, Decide later or Cancelled silences it regardless of the stored date, a Book-by date falling after the item's own date is refused inline, and an item carrying no deadline adds no warning and no UI anywhere |
| Night coverage strip | One cell per night, colored booked / decide later / to book / in transit / no stay (a trip with no stay yet draws every night as "no stay" rather than hiding the strip: those are the nights the uncovered-night warning's "show" rings, so a hidden strip made its own action do nothing); hover for details, click to jump. Every jumpable cell is a real control rather than a painted div: it takes keyboard focus with a visible ring, carries a screen-reader label matching its tooltip, and jumps on Enter or Space exactly as it does on a click, with focus following to the rebuilt cell. A cell with nothing to jump to stays unfocusable, so tabbing never stops on a dead square |
| Fill an uncovered night | A "No stay covers ..." warning is the one line in the panel that names nights rather than an item, so it now acts on them: "show" rings exactly those cells in the night-coverage strip for a couple of seconds, and "Add stay" opens the Add-item form already set to Stay with check-in on the first uncovered night and check-out the morning after the last one, read from the same coverage data the warning and the strip are drawn from, and carrying the CITY those nights are spent in (the stay being checked out of, or wherever a leg landed) so the only field left is which hotel. Saving it covers the gap and the whole line (control included) disappears; a trip with several holes offers one per line, each filling its own range. Cancelling creates nothing. Absent in a read-only shared view, where nothing can be added |
| Overnight legs | Flights and transport carry arrival date + time; overnight legs show a +1d badge and count as covered (booked) nights |
| Shift dates | Move one item, everything after it, or the whole trip by N days in one action |
| Duplicate a day | Each Days-view card copies every item that starts that day to a target date you pick, in one undoable step. Clones keep a "(copy)" title and a fresh id; start and end dates shift by the same delta, so a three-night stay stays three nights, and a blank or invalid end date is carried across unchanged. The source day is left untouched and anything already on the target date is kept, never overwritten; the action lives in the day header's `⋯` menu ("Copy to another date") and is disabled on a day with nothing starting on it |
| Place picker | The Place field on any item, and both fields in the route dialog, autocomplete against Open-Meteo's geocoding endpoint (keyless, built for typeahead; Nominatim forbids it and stays a one-shot lookup). Rows carry a second line that disambiguates namesakes - "Paris, Île-de-France, France" against "Paris, Texas, United States" - which is the whole reason the old `<datalist>` was not enough. Places already used in the open trip are offered first, tagged "in this trip". Ranking is population-dominant on purpose: "tok" means Tokyo, not the village of Tok, Alaska, though the village is still listed second. Only what the traveller typed is stored (the bare city name, which is also the day-card chip and the weather key), but PICKING a row seeds the geocode cache with that row's country and coordinates, so the map, weather and visa lookups skip Nominatim for it and the visa dialog may name the country outright - a human choosing "Nara, Japan" from a list that also offered Nara, Mali is exactly the certainty the confidence gate exists to look for. Typing without picking behaves as it always did |
| Airport picker | Flight items get From/To airport fields above the title, searching 3,271 IATA airports by city, airport name, code or alias. The table ships with the app (OurAirports, public domain, ~260 KB) rather than coming from an API: every airport API needs a secret key, which a static client-side app cannot hold, and a bundled file also works offline and is precached by the service worker. Aliases matter - Narita's municipality is "Narita", so without them a search for "tokyo" would never find NRT, and "new york" would never find EWR. Weak matches are word-anchored: a query under five characters must begin a word somewhere in the city, name or aliases, so "rome" no longer drags in Split (Saint Je-rome), Dover (Aird-rome) and Gustavia (Aé-rod-rome) while "orly" still reaches ORY through the airport name. Five characters and up an interior hit is usually deliberate and still counts ("eathrow"). Picking both writes the title as `City (CODE) to City (CODE)`, the same shape the day cards already parse for a travel day's departure city, so nothing new is stored on the item and a hand-written title is never overwritten. Editing an existing flight re-fills both fields from its title, and a NEW flight opens with From already set to wherever the itinerary has last flown to (right for the next leg of a multi-city trip and right for the flight home, which both leave from where the traveller now is); only that field, so nothing writes a title until the destination is picked |
| Hotel picker | Stay items search real lodging by name as you type: the Title field is already relabelled "Hotel / stay name" for a stay, and it becomes a combobox there and ONLY there (the other five types keep it a plain free-text title, checked per search because the type switches under an open form). Unlike the airport table this cannot be bundled - OpenStreetMap carries over a million lodging POIs against 3,271 airports in 260 KB - so it is a live lookup against Photon, which is the same OSM data, keyless, CORS-open and built for typeahead (Nominatim forbids autocomplete, Open-Meteo resolves settlements and never POIs). Rows show the town and a HOTEL / HOSTEL / GUEST HOUSE / MOTEL / APARTMENT pill. The city already in the Place field outranks Photon's own order, which is what makes "novotel" with "Bangkok" in the form answer Bangkok rather than Christchurch; a bare chain name with no city is the weak spot, and typing the city too fixes it. Photon's `location_bias_scale`, `zoom` and `bbox` were all tried and rejected - the first two made the bias worse and a bbox hard-filters, returning an EMPTY list for a hotel that is simply not in OSM. Only the bare name is stored, but picking a row seeds the geocode cache with THE HOTEL's coordinates instead of the city centroid every stay used to share, which is the real gain: map pins, leg distances and route legs all sharpen. An empty Place field is filled in from the pick; a filled one is left alone. One Google Places call fires on the pick (never per keystroke) to show the same star-rating chip the assistant cards use, and it stays invisible with no key configured |
| Venue picker | Activity items get the same treatment stays get, for the rest of the map: typing three characters into an activity's Title searches real venues by name and a pick fills the title, the Place field (when empty) and the venue's exact coordinates. Rows carry a category pill - Museum, Restaurant, Cafe, Park, Attraction, Castle, Beach, Lake, Station and eighty more - drawn from a curated allowlist of OSM classes, and two rows a traveller could not tell apart (same name, same town, same pill) are one row, so `Eiffel` offers the tower, a cafe and a station rather than six bus stops named Eiffel. Ranking is checked against the live service rather than against fixtures alone, which is how two things that a fixture could not show were found: a landmark and the shop named after it are different rows (the class is part of what makes two results the same result), and a mapped area outranks a point of the same name, so `Sagrada Familia` answers the basilica rather than the ice cream shop across the street. Same provider, debounce and failure posture as the hotel picker (Photon, 320ms, three characters, silent empty list when it is down), and ONE combobox on the field: which list it offers is decided per search, because the type switches under an open form. It is a NAME search and is not sold as anything else: `teamLab` with Tokyo in the Place field answers teamLab Planets, and `museum` answers places literally named Museum, because nothing in the response ranks by category. Lodging is deliberately absent (that is the stay picker's field), and so are cities, streets and buildings. The pick costs nothing: unlike a hotel pick it fires no Google ratings call at all, and the coordinates it stores are what stop a hand-added activity sitting on its city's centroid until some later lookup finds it |
| Food &amp; Drink | Restaurants, bars and cafes are their own top-level type in the item form rather than an `activity` whose CATEGORY the traveller had to type into the title as `Dinner: `. Choosing it reveals one compact Category select (Breakfast, Brunch, Lunch, Dinner, Drinks, Cafe, Snack, Other) and nothing else; every other type sees no trace of the control. The Title field is then the venue's NAME and only that (`Saba`, `Hot Tin`), which is what the place autocomplete searches - previously each keystroke of the `Dinner: ` prefix re-queried Photon with a string no venue is named, so classifying an item meant fighting the place search. Itinerary cards show the icon and the venue name and never repeat the category in words (`🍽️ Saba`, not `🍽️ Dinner: Saba`); the icon carries the category as its accessible name, so a screen reader still hears "Dinner" where a sighted reader sees the glyph, and the amber meal accent is unchanged. The `.ics` export is the one surface that keeps the words (`SUMMARY:Dinner: Saba`) because a calendar entry has no icon to carry them, and CSV gains a `category` column appended after the existing ones. Storage stays `type: activity` plus a `meal` field on purpose (see the data-model note above); a legacy `Dinner: Saba` is migrated to `meal: dinner` + `title: Saba` on load, on import and on share-link import, by the same deterministic split - only the four literal assistant-contract prefixes with a colon, so `Sunset dinner cruise` and `Dinnerware shopping` are never touched. Editing an old item therefore opens on Food &amp; Drink with its category chosen and only the venue in the title, and the assistant's wire contract is unchanged: the model still writes `Dinner: Narisawa`, and the accept path converts at the boundary |
| The form already knows | A blank Add-item form used to open on the same six empty fields whatever the trip contained, so adding the fifth thing to a Kyoto day meant retyping "Kyoto" and picking the date out of a calendar for the fifth time. It now derives what it can from items already entered: the TYPE (Flight on an empty plan, since the first thing anyone logs is how they get there; Activity once the trip holds anything, which is measured rather than assumed: activities are the largest group across the 13 example trips - 41% against 33% for food &amp; drink once that split off into its own type - and all 13 open with a flight), the DATE (the day card's own day when the + came from one, else today while the trip is running, else its first day - and nothing at all on a plan with no dated item, because today is a guess about intent rather than a reading of the itinerary), and the CITY (the stay covering that night, else where a leg LANDS that day, else the last located item at or before it). So logging a flight and then adding a hotel already says the city you are flying into, and a day card's + opens on that day in that day's city. Choosing Stay fills check-in and check-out from the first uncovered night (and on a trip with no stays yet, from the night you LAND rather than the night you left, since an overnight flight is that night's bed - two stacked red-eyes skip two nights, not one), and a blank flight form fills the From airport with wherever the itinerary has already flown to - only that one, so no title is composed on the traveller's behalf until they pick the destination themselves. Every value is a PREFILL, on screen and editable before anything saves, and the form tracks WHO filled each field: a value the app wrote stays revisable (choosing Stay improves on the app's own opening date; moving the date re-derives the city, and clears it on a day that cannot justify one), while a field the traveller has typed in - or filled by picking a row from a dropdown - is never written to again. An edit derives nothing at all (the item's own values are the answer), and a flight or a between-cities transport is never given a city, because those two keep their route in the title |
| Add the missing leg | The "No flight or transport is logged between Paris and Brussels" warning already knew both endpoints and the day, and used to make the traveller restate all three into a blank form. It now carries an "Add transport" beside the jump link, opening the form as a Transport titled `Paris to Brussels` - the same "A to B" shape the app writes and parses everywhere else - dated the day the previous stay ends. Saving it clears the warning that offered it. Absent in a read-only shared view, where nothing can be added |
| Clear filters | A `✕ Clear filters` control sits in the toolbar whenever any filter or search term is active, and resets the search box, the type, status and traveler filters together. It is hidden when nothing is filtered. Previously the only way out of a filter combination was the link inside the "nothing matches" empty state, which never appeared while a filter still matched something |
| Read-only shared view | Opening a `share=` link renders someone else's itinerary and locks every action that would write to YOUR data. Only export trip (JSON / CSV / .ics), share, and the 12/24-hour and Miles/Kilometers toggles stay live in the trip menu; the other ten entries are visibly disabled rather than clickable-but-inert. "Backup all trips" is specifically among them, because in a shared view it produced a file named `trip-planner-backup.json` containing only the stranger's trip, which would have looked like total data loss on restore. The trip picker, the status pickers and the currency picker are disabled too, the cross-trip search is hidden entirely, and the `n` keyboard shortcut no longer opens the Add-item dialog |
| Read a booking PDF | `📄 Read a booking PDF` in the trip menu takes a flight or hotel confirmation - a PDF, or pasted text - and offers the items it found as ordinary proposal cards. Deterministic, not AI: airport codes resolve against the bundled table, and dates, times, booking reference and price are read by pattern. **The file is never uploaded and never stored**; it is read into memory, turned into text, and dropped, so nothing lands in the documents pocket. Every reading shows the LINE it came from and a confidence, because a wrong read has to be visible rather than merely plausible in a form field. Accepting one goes through the same validation, undo and save path as an assistant suggestion. Its one extra privilege is transcription provenance: a confirmation code and a `booked` status survive from a document (they were copied, not invented) where they are stripped from a model, and a transcribed price counts as real cost rather than an estimate. Dates are the delicate part: `08/12/2027` is read month-first by default, but a decisive date elsewhere on the page overrides that, and failing one, the reading that produces a possible trip wins - a confirmation whose dates imply a 31-day flight is being read the wrong way round. European document formats are read too: comma-decimal money ("EUR 148,00", "1.234,56 EUR") parses as the price it is rather than a hundredfold of it, a dotted date ("12.08.2027") or a price line is never mistaken for a clock time (a mistake that used to fabricate an overnight leg), and a "+1 800..." customer-service phone number no longer reads as a next-day arrival marker. Booking references require the letter inside the code itself (so a date or an all-digit number next to the label is never a PNR), and uppercase day/month abbreviations that double as real airport codes (SAT, SUN, AUG) are ignored for bare-code route sniffing - the parenthesised "(SAT)" form still matches, so a genuine San Antonio flight keeps its route. Whichever settled it is stated on screen (and the day-order note only appears when an all-numeric date is actually on the page). A connection printed as repeated Depart/Arrive blocks (the airline "My Trips" layout) reads EVERY leg as its own flight - route, date, both times, flight number and seat per leg - instead of collapsing six flights into one; leg dates printed without a year ("Tue, Dec 29") resolve against the document's own full dates, exactly when one names the same day, otherwise by the only year that fits the document's dated span, and a date neither rule settles is left blank with a warning rather than guessed. An arrival date printed on the page always beats the wrapped-clock overnight inference, so an eastbound date-line crossing that lands earlier than it took off stays same-day. PDF text comes from pdf.js, vendored under `vendor/pdfjs/` like Leaflet and loaded only when a PDF is actually chosen (1.7 MB, deliberately NOT precached: the runtime cache picks it up after first use). It parses in-process rather than in a worker - a confirmation is one or two pages and takes about 10 ms, and skipping the worker removes a class of environment-dependent failure. A forty-line built-in reader remains as an offline fallback. A PDF that opens fine but contains no actual text (a scan, or a printer-driver "print to PDF" that draws every letter as shapes) is diagnosed as a picture of text: the dialog explains that no app can copy text out of such a file and points to the source email or booking page instead, rather than sending anyone off to try select-and-copy in a viewer where it cannot work |
| Route map | Numbered stops in visit order connected by a dashed route line (Leaflet + OSM, dark-mode tiles) |
| How to get there | Between consecutive stays in different places: distance (in your chosen unit), compass heading, international-border flag, per-mode duration estimates, and pre-filled links to Google transit/driving, Google Flights and Rome2Rio. Opened from a leg it answers that leg straight away; opened from the toolbar it no longer opens two empty fields on a trip that already knows which two cities have nothing between them - it fills them from the trip (the first city change with no leg logged, else the first city change at all) and leaves the lookup UN-run with focus on the Check button, because a dialog merely opening is not a reason to spend a geocode |
| Import / export | JSON per trip, full backup of all trips, CSV export (includes per-item currency + converted columns, plus confirmation, travelers, bookBy and paymentMethod columns appended in that order so older spreadsheets keep their column order, the payment column carrying the picker's own wording rather than the stored token); import accepts trip files or full backups and re-normalizes traveler names against the trip roster. Attached documents are never included in exports |
| Calendar export | Export trip (.ics) in the trip menu: stays as all-day ranges, and ANY item carrying a clock time as a floating local-time event (overnight arrivals land on the right day) - a 7:30 PM dinner is a 7:30 PM entry rather than the all-day banner it used to be, and an untimed item is still all-day, which is the honest rendering of "some time that day". Every event carries the DTSTAMP the spec requires and lines fold at 75 octets without splitting a character; cancelled items excluded; imports into Google/Apple Calendar. A confirmation code rides along as a `Ref:` line in the event description, since the calendar entry is what a traveler opens at the gate, and a Book-by date and a payment method join it as `Book by:` (spelled out, not ISO) and `Payment:` lines whenever they are set |
| Calendar import | The Read a booking PDF dialog also reads .ics calendar files (file or paste, indented/quoted pastes included): each event becomes an approve-before-adding proposal card; recurring events import their first occurrence and say so |
| Route export (GPX) | Export route (GPX) in the trip menu writes one waypoint per located stop in visit order, for offline map apps; greyed out with an explanatory tooltip until the trip holds at least two items with a Place. It RESOLVES the coordinates it needs when clicked (through the shared, rate-limited geocoder, with a progress toast, and free for anything already cached), so which views you have opened no longer decides whether an export works - it used to spend only the cache the Map view happened to have filled, and said so in its own tooltip |
| Copy a day | "Copy day as text" in each Days-view card's `⋯` menu copies that day as message-ready text: emoji date header, timed rows with type icons, untimed rows bulleted, the night's stay last; the menu item flashes a checkmark (the menu stays open for exactly this action so the flash is seen) and covered-but-empty nights copy their "Staying at" line |
| Spend over time | A weekly-bars block beside Cost by type shows WHEN the booked money goes, on trips spanning two-plus calendar weeks |

An open candidate carries its own compact hours line and adds without interrogation. The prompt also tells the model to respect hours, but that is defence-in-depth: the deterministic check is what stands between a hallucinated schedule and the trip. ## Site assistant setup (owner)

Tier 3 (the free shared assistant) is served by the Netlify function
`netlify/functions/tp-assist.mjs`, which proxies Google Gemini behind per-client
and global daily rate limits. Env vars are not injected into functions on this
site, so the shared key lives in a Blob, written once out-of-band:

```
# 1. Get a free Gemini API key at https://aistudio.google.com/apikey
# 2. Point the CLI at the project that serves shevato.com (blob stores are
#    per-project, so writing this while linked elsewhere silently does nothing):
netlify status
# 3. Store it (the key never travels over HTTP; it is set via the CLI):
netlify blobs:set trip-planner-assist config '{"geminiKey":"<key>"}'
# Disable the shared assistant again:
netlify blobs:set trip-planner-assist config '{}'
```

With no key set the endpoint returns `503 not_configured` and the UI tells the
traveller to use Copy & paste or bring their own key ("Tier 1/2/3" is internal
shorthand only; the UI names the modes Free assistant / Copy & paste / My API
key, listed in that order). Tiers 1 and 2 need no setup.

Two failure modes look alike from the browser but are not: `503 not_configured`
means the key is missing from *this* project's store, while `502 upstream` means
the key was found and the upstream call failed: either Gemini rejected it (most
often a retired `GEMINI_MODEL` pin, see the note in `tp-assist.mjs`) or the call
outran the function's own upstream deadline (`ASSIST_UPSTREAM_TIMEOUT_MS`, 45s;
a plan-mode turn legitimately runs 8-14s, so this only fires on a genuinely hung
upstream). A `429` means a quota was hit, either this function's own daily
limits or Google's. The function logs the upstream status and body on an HTTP
error and the error name/message on a timeout or network failure; the key is
never logged.

**Google's free tier is the real ceiling, not our limiter.** Measured
2026-07-19 on a free key: `gemini-3.5-flash` allows 5 requests/minute and
250K tokens/minute, and the API refuses further calls once a
`generate_content_free_tier_requests` allowance of about 20 (apparently daily)
is spent. This function's own caps (10/client/hour, 30/client/day, 400/day
global, in `lib/tp-assist-quota.mjs`) are far above that, so on the free tier
travellers hit Google's wall first and see "at capacity". Check the real numbers
at https://aistudio.google.com/rate-limit; enabling billing on the Google Cloud
project raises the limits and costs fractions of a cent per turn.

### How a rating gets onto a row (and the opening hours with it)

Because a rating may not be stored and costs $0.02 every time it is fetched, the
client is careful about what it asks for and when. One queue owns every billed
request (`createPlacesQueue` in trip-logic.js):

- **Demand follows the eye.** An itinerary row registers its venue with an
  `IntersectionObserver` and is looked up when it comes within 600px of the
  viewport, so opening a 50-place trip costs the handful of venues on the first
  screen rather than fifty. Assistant candidate cards are the exception and are
  fetched at once: they are a comparison the traveller asked for, they arrive
  together in an open panel, and the winner badges are a judgement across the
  whole set. There is deliberately no background sweep of the rest of the trip.
- **A venue is reserved the moment it is planned**, not when its batch reaches
  the wire, so no number of re-renders, view switches or scrolls can put the
  same venue on the wire twice.
- **Batches are capped at 12** (the server's limit) and at most 2 are in flight
  at once, so a large trip arrives progressively instead of as a burst.
- **The session cache is the whole cache.** Results live in memory for the life
  of the page and are never written anywhere; Timeline, Days, the stay picker
  and the assistant all read the same one, so switching views or trips repaints
  for free.
- **Opening hours ride the same response.** `regularOpeningHours` and
  `currentOpeningHours` are Place Details Enterprise fields - the exact SKU the
  rating already bills - so the field mask asks for them in the SAME request and
  the price of a lookup does not move. One response feeds the rating chip, the
  coordinates, the Days-view hours line and the assistant's closed-venue check;
  nothing about hours ever makes a second request, and a view switch repaints
  hours from the session cache like everything else. Like the rating, hours
  have no caching exception in Google's terms, so they are never persisted
  anywhere - the session cache is their whole life.
- **A 429 parks the queue, it does not empty it.** The response says which
  bucket rejected the batch and when it refills (`scope` + `resetAt` +
  `Retry-After`), the queue waits exactly that long and then serves the same
  venues. Retries are bounded, and a rejection that will not clear for hours is
  not re-asked every few seconds.
- **Degrading is quiet.** A venue with no rating is an ordinary `Google Maps`
  link, exactly as it looks when no key is configured. If the allowance is gone
  for long enough to matter, the app says so once in a toast rather than putting
  an error badge on forty rows.

### The monthly budget (why ratings can stop, on purpose)

A rating is a Place Details Enterprise call: **1,000 free per calendar month
per Google project, then $0.02 each**. `MONTHLY_BUDGET` in
`lib/tp-places-quota.mjs` is a single **850-call ceiling shared by every tier**,
so owner and public traffic draw on one pot and cannot add up to a bill between
them. It is enforced inside the same atomic reservation the rest of the quota
uses, before any call to Google, and it resets at 08:00Z on the 1st (never
earlier than Google's own reset).

850 rather than 1,000 because our counter is not provably equal to Google's
billed number - see FINDINGS for the August 2026 reconciliation - so 15% is
held back. At the ceiling the cost is $0.00.

When it is exhausted the endpoint answers `429 {scope:"free_month"}` with
`Retry-After`; the client parks for the rest of the month, every row keeps its
plain `Google Maps` link, and the traveller is told once. Check where the month
stands with an owner-gated status read:

```
curl -s -H "X-TP-Owner-Token: <token>" -H "Origin: https://shevato.com" \
  "https://shevato.com/.netlify/functions/tp-places?status=1"
```

Local `netlify dev` will NOT spend against the real card on a key alone: it also
needs `TP_PLACES_ALLOW_LOCAL_SPEND=1`, and the production key is no longer in
`.env` at all. For paid local testing, create a SEPARATE dev key in Google Cloud
restricted to `places.googleapis.com`.

**The credential lives in exactly one place**: the `trip-planner-places` config
blob, under `placesKeyV2`. The field name is a version gate - every function
version deployed before that field existed reads `placesKey`, so old Netlify
deploy permalinks (which stay live forever and would otherwise run pre-guard
code against the live blob) resolve nothing and answer 503. Google also enforces
a **500/day** cap on `GetPlaceRequest` for the whole project, which bounds any
path that somehow avoids the guard.
